Where the lanes wentDesign choices

Kerbs, not paint

Every new metre is physically separated from traffic.

Painted lanes were cheaper but did not change who rode. Counts on the two painted corridors rose 4%; on kerbed corridors they rose 61%.

The extra cost was about 18% per kilometre, mostly drainage work that the roads budget had already scheduled.

Who started ridingFindings

Three groups drove the growth

  1. Shift workers at the hospital, who had no bus before 6am.
  2. Secondary pupils from the southern estates, now that the route is continuous.
  3. Over-60s on e-bikes from the share scheme, up fivefold.
Source: rider survey, n = 2,1409

AppendixMethod notes

How the figures were produced

Counters

Trips come from 41 automatic counters. Each counter records passing bikes with an induction loop; we exclude days when a counter was offline for more than two hours and fill those days from the same weekday in the surrounding fortnight.

Survey

The rider survey ran for four weeks in October. Riders were approached at nine sites at different times of day, including two night sessions at the hospital. Responses are weighted to match the counter totals by site.

Costs

Costs are outturn figures from the capital programme, plus the first full year of running costs for the share scheme. They exclude staff time from the transport office.

Limits

Counters miss riders on streets without one, so the city-wide total is an estimate. Year-on-year comparisons use only the 33 counters that ran for both years.
